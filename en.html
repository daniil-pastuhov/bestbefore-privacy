BestBefore
Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

BestBefore keeps your pantry on your phone. You need no account, and there is no advertising and no analytics. Apart from crash reports (see “Crash reports”), we receive data only from people who buy recipes, and only once they start a purchase (see “Buying recipes”). If you do not buy recipes, nothing changes for you. We do not sell your personal data.

Who is responsible

The data controller is Daniil Pastuhov, Norway: the individual developer who publishes BestBefore and is responsible for the data described in this policy. To reach him, see “Contact”.

What the app stores

Your pantry items, expiry dates, shopping list, settings (including your allergen list and cooking preferences), product photos, saved recipes and any shop locations you save are stored only in the app's local database on your device. They are not sent to us. The one exception is a recipe request from our server, which you send yourself; see “Recipes from our server”.

Camera

The camera is used to scan barcodes, read printed expiry dates and photograph food items. Recognition runs on your device (Google ML Kit on Android, Apple Vision on iOS). Photos you take are saved only on your device.

Internet requests

When you scan a barcode, the app asks Open Food Facts for the product's name and picture. That request contains the barcode number, and, as with any web request, Open Food Facts' servers can see your IP address. No name, account or device identifier is added by the app. Product pictures are also loaded from Open Food Facts. See their privacy policy.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, so we can find and fix the bug. A report contains the technical details of the crash (the stack trace), the device model, the operating system and app versions, free memory and disk space at the time, and a random installation ID that Firebase creates to group reports from the same installation. It contains no pantry items, photos, locations, name or account. Crash reports are not used for advertising or tracking, and are kept for 90 days. See Firebase's privacy information.

Location (optional)

Location is off by default. If you turn on shop reminders, you can save a shop's position and the app shows your shopping list when you are near it. Saved positions stay on your device. On Android, nearby-shop detection is performed by Google Play services on your device; see Google's privacy policy. You can turn the feature off, or revoke the permission, at any time.

Notifications, backups and sharing

  • Expiry reminders and digests are scheduled locally on your device.
  • Exporting a backup or sharing a shopping list or recipe idea only happens when you choose to, and goes where you send it.
  • Your phone's own backup (Google or iCloud) may include the app's data. That is controlled by your device settings, not by us.

Recipes

The Recipe tab can suggest a recipe from the items you tick, in three ways:

  • Share or copy a prompt (free). The app writes a text and hands it to the app you choose, or copies it. We receive nothing. What happens next is up to that app and its own privacy policy.
  • On this phone (free). On supported phones, the phone's own model writes the recipe (Gemini Nano on Android, Apple Foundation Models on iPhone). Nothing is sent to us.
  • From our server (paid). You buy recipes in Google Play or the App Store, and our server writes each recipe or three-day menu.

The next six sections apply only if you start a purchase of recipes.

Buying recipes

You buy recipes through Google Play or the App Store. We never see your payment details. Our server checks each purchase with the store and keeps a record of it: the store, the product, the quantity, the number of recipes, whether it was a test purchase, the dates, its state (added, used, refunded), the store's order or transaction number, and the buyer identifiers described under “Refunds”. On Google Play, the store's purchase token is kept only until the purchase is completed; after that, only a scrambled (hashed) form of it stays, as the record's name.

Our server runs on Google Firebase: Authentication, Cloud Functions, Firestore and App Check. Cloud Functions and Firestore run in Google's EU region europe-west1 (Belgium).

When you start a purchase, the app creates an anonymous identifier on our server (a random Firebase user ID). It holds your recipe balance and the numbers of recipes bought, used and refunded. It is not linked to your name or email. When you buy, the app gives the store a code made from this identifier, so that the purchase and any refund can be matched to your balance.

Signing in is optional. You can sign in with Apple or Google on iPhone, and with Google on Android, so your recipes can be used on other phones. Sign in with Apple is not available on Android: to use an account made with Apple on an Android phone, add Google sign-in to it in the iPhone app first. We then keep the account identifier from Apple or Google and your email address (with Sign in with Apple, this may be Apple's relay address). We do not ask for your name: with Sign in with Apple, the app asks Apple only for your email address. With Sign in with Google, Google also passes the name and profile picture link of your Google account to Firebase Authentication. Our server removes them from your account's main record after you sign in, but Firebase Authentication keeps a copy in the record of your Google sign-in until your account is deleted, and we cannot remove that copy separately. We do not use them.

If you sign in on a phone that holds recipes under an anonymous identifier, and you agree, those recipes move to your account and the anonymous identifier is deleted. A record that they were moved, and to which account, stays, so that a later refund reaches the right account.

Recipes from our server

When you ask our server for a recipe or a three-day menu, the app sends:

  • the names, best-before dates and quantities of the items you ticked (at most 30);
  • your cooking preferences: difficulty, level of detail, servings, time, meal, diet and language;
  • the allergen list from Settings;
  • today's date and a random request number.

Our server passes the items, preferences, allergens and date to Google's Gemini model on Vertex AI, which writes the recipe. No name, email or device identifier is sent to the model. We use Vertex AI's “global” endpoint, so Google may process the request outside the EU/EEA. Google processes it as our service provider under its Google Cloud terms; see Google's Vertex AI data governance page. Your allergen list is used only to tell the model what to leave out and to check the answer; it is not stored.

Our server checks requests and answers automatically, to stop the model being misused: it refuses item or allergen names that do not look like food names, and answers that contain, for example, links, contact details, an allergen from your list or text that is not about cooking. A refused name or answer is not stored or logged. Our server counts refused attempts under your identifier for the current day and logs the rule that refused them with a scrambled (hashed) form of your identifier; after several in one day, recipe requests from your account are refused until the next day.

We do not log or keep what you send. Our server keeps only a fingerprint (a hash) of the request, so that a repeated request can be recognised and is not charged twice. The recipe or menu written back, which names the ingredients it uses, is kept on our server for 7 days, so that the app can still collect it if the connection is lost. Then it is deleted automatically (Google's automatic deletion usually runs within a day after that). The app saves the recipe on your phone, where it stays until you delete it.

Technical data for buyers

  • App Check. With each request to our server, the app proves that it is a genuine copy, through Firebase App Check: with Google Play Integrity on Android and Apple App Attest on iPhone. This starts only when you start a purchase.
  • IP address. Our server uses your IP address to limit how many requests one source can send in a minute. It counts requests per address for the current minute in memory only, and does not store the address. Google's infrastructure also records each request to our server, with its IP address, in our server's technical logs.
  • Usage counts. For each recipe request, our server logs one line with a scrambled (hashed) form of your identifier, the kind of request, how much text the model read and wrote (tokens), the result and the estimated cost. The line never contains your items, allergens, the prompt or the recipe. Our server also keeps, under your identifier, the number of requests, refused attempts and the estimated cost in the current minute and day, to apply limits.
  • Daily totals. Our server keeps daily totals for all users together (requests, recipes, estimated cost), with no user data.
  • Technical logs are kept by Google Cloud Logging for 30 days.
  • Crash reports, as described in “Crash reports”.

Refunds

Google Play and the App Store tell our server when a purchase is refunded, and the refunded recipes are taken off your balance. If you had already used them, your balance can go below zero.

On the App Store, the purchase sheet tells you before you buy that if you ask Apple for a refund, we tell Apple how many of the recipes you have used. For a purchase made after you saw this, we record that you saw it, and when. If you then ask Apple for a refund, Apple asks us about the purchase, and we tell Apple that the recipes were delivered and what share of the purchase you have used. For other purchases we tell Apple nothing.

If purchases are refunded again and again after the recipes were used, buying recipes is switched off for that buyer; recipes already bought can still be used. To do this, each purchase is recorded with buyer identifiers: on the App Store, an identifier Apple gives this app for your Apple account (its app transaction ID); on Google Play, your identifier on our server and a scrambled (hashed) form of each Apple or Google account identifier linked to your account, if any. When a refund takes recipes that were already used, we keep a record of it under those identifiers: the purchase, the date, the store and the number of used recipes refunded.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account in the app: Recipe tab → Recipe settings → Delete account. If you are signed in, you sign in once more first. Then:

  • your sign-in and email address are deleted from our server (with Sign in with Google, also the copy of the Google name and picture link that Firebase Authentication kept). If your account uses Sign in with Apple and you delete it in the iPhone app, the app also asks Apple to revoke its access to your Apple Account. The Android app has no Sign in with Apple and cannot do this: if you delete such an account on Android, you can remove the link yourself in your Apple Account settings, under Sign in with Apple;
  • the recipes and menus kept on our server, and the per-minute and per-day counts, are deleted;
  • your unused recipes are lost and cannot be refunded.

What stays, and why:

  • every purchase record, no longer linked to your account. It holds no name or email, but it keeps your old identifier, the buyer identifiers (see “Refunds”) and the store's order or transaction number, which Apple or Google can link to you. It is the only way to stop one store purchase being added twice, and we need it to handle later refunds and for our accounts;
  • a closed record under your old identifier, with the date of deletion, the number of recipes lost and the totals of recipes bought, used and refunded, the counts behind the refund and misuse limits, and the buyer identifiers. It stops a deleted account being used again, and keeps our accounts correct;
  • a record of refunds after use, if there is one (see “Refunds”), to prevent fraud. It is not deleted;
  • technical logs until they expire after 30 days, and the daily totals, which hold no user data.

Deleting the app does not delete anything on our server. If you never signed in, we cannot tell which identifier was yours, so we cannot find or delete it for you later, and its unused recipes are lost. Saved recipes and your pantry stay on your phone until you delete them.

Who receives data, and where

  • Google, as our service provider: Firebase (Authentication, App Check, Cloud Functions, Firestore, Crashlytics), Google Cloud Logging and Vertex AI (Gemini). Google Play handles purchases and refunds on Android, and Play Integrity checks the app.
  • Apple: the App Store handles purchases and refunds on iPhone, and receives the information described under “Refunds” if you ask Apple for a refund; App Attest checks the app; Sign in with Apple on iPhone, if you use it.
  • Open Food Facts, for barcode lookups, as described above.

We do not show ads, do not use analytics and do not sell your data. Cloud Functions and Firestore are in the EU. Vertex AI's global endpoint, Firebase Authentication, App Check and Crashlytics may process data outside the EU/EEA, for example in the United States. Google covers such transfers in its data processing terms, which include the EU's standard contractual clauses.

Legal bases

Under the GDPR, we rely on:

  • contract (Article 6(1)(b)): to check your purchases, hold your recipes and your sign-in, and write the recipes you ask for;
  • legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)): crash reports, App Check, rate limits, usage counts, the automatic checks of requests and answers, refund records and answering Apple's questions about a refund, to keep the service working and to prevent fraud and abuse; and keeping purchase records after an account is deleted, so that one purchase cannot be used twice;
  • legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)): keeping the records we need for accounting.

Children

The app is not directed at children. Apart from the crash reports and, for people who buy recipes, the data described above, we do not knowingly collect data from anyone.

Your control and your rights

You can edit or delete any item inside the app. Uninstalling the app deletes its data from your device. If you bought recipes, you can delete your account as described in “Deleting your account”.

You have the right to ask for a copy of your data, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict or object to its use, and to complain to the data protection authority where you live (in Norway, Datatilsynet). To use these rights, contact us (see “Contact”). If you never signed in, we have no way to tell which data is yours.
